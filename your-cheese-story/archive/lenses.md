# Narrative Lenses

*Used in the warm run. Each lens is one way of looking at the same facts. For each lens, judge how much real material the Creamery Card holds (strong, some, or thin), say in one line what story that lens would tell, and name the facts behind it. A thin lens gets the one question that would unlock it.*

A lens is not a section of a story. The final story usually leans on one or two lenses as its spine and borrows details from others. The point of running all twelve is to see which ones the facts actually support, and to catch a strong one nobody expected.

## The lenses

**1. The maker's personal story.** Who makes the cheese, what they did before, how cheese pulled them in, what was hard. Needs: the maker's own account. Thin when all you have is a name and a title.

**2. The farm or creamery.** The actual place and how it came to be what it is: when it started, what changed, what the buildings were before. Needs: dated, specific history. Weak version: "a family farm." Strong version: what the family did, when, and why.

**3. The animals.** Species, breed, herd size, how they are kept, what they eat by season, whether they have names. Needs: facts about this herd, not the breed in general. Breed history from the kit is context only.

**4. The milk and the season.** How the milk changes through the year and what the maker does about it. Spring pasture, winter hay, a seasonal cheese made only part of the year. Needs: what the maker notices and adjusts. This lens is often where taste, place, and craft meet.

**5. The development of a particular cheese.** How one cheese came to be: the recipe it started from, what failed, what they changed, why it is the way it is now. Needs: the maker's account of trial and change.

**6. The deep history of the style.** The tradition the cheese belongs to: where the style came from, what problem it originally solved, how it was made. Needs: kit background plus confirmation of how the maker actually relates to that tradition. Never claim the maker follows a historical method unless they say so.

**7. The tradition reinvented.** How the maker uses an old tradition in a new way, for new people, in a different place. Needs: the specific departures the maker chose. Often strong for Vermont makers working in European styles.

**8. The accident or the failure.** The batch that went wrong and taught them something, the cheese that started as a mistake, the year everything changed. Needs: the maker telling it. Never invent one. Ask: "What went wrong once, and what did you learn?"

**9. Survival.** The economics under the farm: selling milk at whatever price the market set, the decline of small dairies, cheese as the way the farm stayed alive. Needs: the maker's own confirmation that this is true of them. Don't borrow the general Vermont dairy story and assume it applies.

**10. The land.** What this ground was and is: the valley, the soil, the river, what grew here before. Needs: specific, sourced facts about this land, ideally from the maker.

**11. The name.** Why the cheese or the creamery is called what it is. Often a person, a place, or a joke. Needs: the maker's explanation. Small, but excellent for counters and labels.

**12. The people around it.** Neighbors, partners, mentors, relatives running nearby businesses, the brewery whose beer washes the rind, the cellar that ages the wheels. Needs: named, confirmed relationships.

## Judging strength

- **Strong:** several confirmed facts that connect, at least one vivid detail, ideally something the maker said in their own words.
- **Some:** a couple of facts, or good facts that still need confirming.
- **Thin:** a single fact, only general background, or only your inference.

## What to return for each lens

- `strength`: strong, some, or thin.
- `one_line`: the story this lens would tell, in one plain sentence. For thin lenses, the best you can honestly say.
- `material_fact_ids`: the facts it rests on.
- `unlock_question` (thin or some only): the single concrete question that would most strengthen it.

## Choosing the story

After the lenses, propose the strongest unifying story: the thread that makes several strong lenses explain one another. Start from what the maker said they want people to understand, and honor every preference they've given. If the strongest material points somewhere else, offer it alongside their framing.

Give a one-line version a monger could repeat, and a paragraph. Then do the same for each cheese, where the material supports it. A cheese with thin material gets a short honest version and its unlock questions, not an inflated story.
