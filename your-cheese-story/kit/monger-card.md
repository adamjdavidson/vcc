# Monger Card

**Purpose:** One printable card per cheese that gives a cheesemonger what they need to sell it at the counter, in the time it takes to cut a sample.

## Who reads it and how

A monger reads it once, maybe pins it behind the counter, and then talks from memory. They are cheese professionals: they know the vocabulary, taste hundreds of cheeses, and want specifics. They need one line they can say while slicing, and a few facts for the customer who leans in.

## What it needs from the Creamery Card

The cheese's name, style, milk, rind, aging, and format; the chosen story for this cheese; maker-confirmed tasting language; facts about the herd, milk, and make that explain the price; and how the name is pronounced, if that's a question.

## Structure

1. **The counter line** (one sentence, under 25 words). The story a monger can say out loud while slicing. Specific, repeatable, true.
2. **What it is** (one line). Milk, style, rind, age, format. Technical vocabulary is welcome.
3. **Taste** (two to four short notes). From the maker or a published source. If the Card has none: `[ask: what do you and your customers taste in it?]`
4. **Try it with** (two suggestions). Label these as suggestions. They may come from general knowledge of the style.
5. **Why it costs what it does** (two or three sentences). Built only from facts about this cheese and farm: herd size, how the milk is handled, aging time, hand work. Follow the kit's guidance on explaining premium prices: specific and unbothered, never apologetic. If the Card lacks those facts: `[ask: what goes into this cheese that a customer can't see?]`
6. **Say it** (only if the name could trip someone up). How to pronounce it, from the maker.
7. **Ask the customer** (one line). A question that starts a conversation at the counter, like "Do you like a cheese that's stronger in the nose than on the tongue?"
8. **What changes** (optional, one line). How it varies by season or batch, if the maker has said.

Total: under 150 words. It must fit on one card.

## What must come from the maker

Tasting notes, the price explanation, the pronunciation, and anything about variation. Everything in the card except the pairings is public-facing and follows the evidence rules: only maker-confirmed or sourced facts, gaps where something is missing.

## Weak and strong

Weak (fictional Birch Hollow Creamery):
> **Hollow Tomme.** A handcrafted artisan cheese made with passion on our family farm. Rich, nutty, and complex with a buttery finish. Pairs with wine. Worth every penny.

Strong:
> **Hollow Tomme**
> *Counter line:* Made only from May to October, when the farm's 22 Jerseys are on pasture, so each wheel is a slice of one summer.
> *What it is:* Raw cow's milk, natural rind, aged 5 to 8 months, 4-lb wheels.
> *Taste:* Brown butter, cut grass, a little mushroom near the rind. (Maker's notes.)
> *Try it with (suggestions):* Dry cider. Rye bread with honey.
> *Why it costs what it does:* One small herd, milked twice a day, and a cheese they only make half the year. Each wheel is turned by hand every week for at least five months.
> *Ask the customer:* "Do you like your cheese earthy or sweet?"
> *What changes:* `[ask: how do early-summer wheels differ from late-summer ones?]`

## Self-check

- Could a monger repeat the counter line after reading it once?
- Is every taste note and price fact traceable to the maker or a source?
- Are pairings clearly marked as suggestions?
- Under 150 words?
