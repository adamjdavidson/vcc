# Story Arc Best Cases

*Real cheeses that show each story arc done well, with sources. The arcs guide (`kit/v2/arcs-guide.md`) carries a short "Best cases" list under each arc; this file has the longer notes and the links.*

**How to use these.** Best cases are models of shape, never material. They show the AI and the maker what a strong version of an arc looks like: which elements carry it, how specific the details get, where the story turns. No fact, name or phrase from a best case is ever carried into another maker's story. When a maker's own cheese appears here (von Trapp, Grafton, Jasper Hill), their entry is a model for other makers; their own story is still built from what they say in Gathering, and checked at the fact check like anyone else's.

**On accuracy.** Each entry rests on the sources listed with it. Sources sometimes disagree (on whether a cheese is raw or pasteurized, on aging times, on the herd). Where they do, the entry says so or leaves the detail out. Anything used in a real maker's story gets confirmed with the maker at the fact check.

## The Make (new)

*For mongers, buyers and obsessive eaters. One cheese, every decision, and what each decision does.*

**Jasper Hill's cheese pages: the spec header.** Every Jasper Hill cheese page opens the same way: milk type, style, milk source, format, affinage, typical profile. Then come the story, the sensory notes, and pairing and service. A monger finds what they need in the same place every time. The technical lines come with reasons. Alpha Tolman's page says the cheese is now made with "Sax Rennet," a traditional rennet built from cultures from previous makes and calf vells, and a distributor sheet adds that natural cultures do the acidifying and coagulating, which Jasper Hill turns into a pairing idea (natural wines). Alpha's curds are cooked and pressed for a tight, elastic texture, and the young wheels are washed with a cultured brine. Each choice is paired with its result.
- Alpha Tolman: https://jasperhillfarm.com/alpha
- Alpha Tolman distributor sheet: https://data.attribytes.com/products/sell_sheet/by_distributor_and_sku/gourmet-foods-international/28866.pdf
- Harbison: https://www.jasperhillfarm.com/harbison

**Jasper Hill's service notes.** Winnimere's page tells retailers that a wheel cut for retail should be sold before the day ends. It's a line only a professional needs, and it signals that the maker knows the counter.
- https://www.jasperhillfarm.com/winnimere

**Cabot Clothbound Black Label: batch as data.** Jasper Hill's sensory team grades every batch of every cheese it makes or ages, which retailers say is part of why mongers trust them. For Cabot Clothbound, more than a decade of batch-by-batch tasting produced four flavor profiles, and statistical analysis assigns each batch to one. The best batches are released as Black Label. Saxelby sold one with its make date, its profile (umami, caramel, roasted), texture, dominant and subtle flavors, and a spider graph. At a co-op tasting, two batches made a day apart tasted drastically different. That's variation sold as a virtue rather than apologized for.
- https://www.saxelbycheese.com/collections/cheese/products/cabot-clothbound-black-label-cheddar
- https://saxelbycheese.com/pages/library-of-cheese-c
- https://dibruno.com/blogs/blog/producer-spotlight-jasper-hill-farm-and-the-cellars-at-jasper-hill
- https://www.citymarket.coop/blog/2022/04/18/exploring-taste-terroir-jasper-hill

**The Comté aroma wheel: shared vocabulary.** 83 descriptors grouped in six families: lactic, fruity, roasted, vegetal, animal, spicy. A ripe Comté typically shows two or three families and five to ten descriptors. The Comté association gives it to retailers as a laminated tasting tool, alongside a trade handout on buying, storing, cutting and selling. Every monger gets the same words.
- https://cheesenotes.com/post/81996274582/comte-updates-wheel-of-aromas/amp
- https://comte-usa.com/trade-resources/pos-materials/

**Pleasant Ridge Reserve: the precise departure.** Uplands learned Beaufort with cheese experts at the University of Wisconsin and then changed the recipe for the wheel size: a traditional Beaufort is roughly 90 pounds, Pleasant Ridge about 10. That one change is the kind of lineage-and-departure detail mongers love.
- https://cheesemaking.com/blogs/fun-along-the-whey/pleasant-hill-reserve

**Vermont worked example: Savage (von Trapp Farmstead).** Cheesemaker Molly Semler describes Savage as a marriage between a cheddar and a traditional Alpine like Comté or Gruyère. It is hard-cooked and pressed, the Alpine way, and dry salted, the cheddar way. Tasters find both: Culture describes it as bold and sharp like cheddar, with sweet, nutty flavors and small crystals; Murray's tastes caramelized onion, beef broth and pâté. It has been aged in Jasper Hill's Alpine vaults and, more recently, in Murray's caves in New York, where green wheels get ten months of brine washes.

What a full Make story still needs from the maker: which cultures, how hot the cook, whether the curd is milled before salting, and why they chose to salt it like a cheddar. Sources disagree on raw versus pasteurized milk and on the herd's breeds (Murray's lists Normande, Montbéliarde and Ayrshire; Culture says mostly Jersey). Settle those with the maker.

The chemistry, as general background for this kind of cheese (not a claim about Savage): cheddar is made with cultures that like moderate heat and a gentle cook; acidity builds, the curd is stacked and milled, and it's salted dry, so it loses calcium and turns short, crumbly and tangy. Alpine cheeses use heat-loving cultures, small curds, a hot cook, pressing while the curd is still sweet, and salting in brine. They keep more calcium, so they're elastic and melt well, and those cultures break down protein over a long aging into brothy flavors and crunchy crystals.
- https://culturecheesemag.com/stories/a-vermont-legacy-continued-in-curd/
- https://culturecheesemag.com/cheese-library/Savage
- https://www.murrayscheese.com/savage
- https://www.murrayscheese.com/dp/murray-s-cave-aged-reserve-savage
- https://cheesenotes.com/post/80729203366/a-visit-to-von-trapp-farmstead/amp

**Where mongers learn this.** Cheese Science Toolkit (cheesescience.org) is written for people who work with cheese after it's made: mongers, buyers, and "curd nerds." It sits between blogs and textbooks, and its author has taught a science course with the Vermont Cheese Council. Bronwen Percival of Neal's Yard Dairy co-wrote *Reinventing the Wheel* and calls the technical side of cheese "the third rail," the barrier to understanding that's worth crossing. Both show the level a Make story can aim for.
- https://www.cheesescience.org/about.html
- https://culturecheesemag.com/cheese-talk/voicings-bronwen-percival

## The Reinvention (new)

*For the adventurous, curious eater. An old cheese rethought here, and what the new idea lets you taste.*

**Winnimere (Jasper Hill).** A take on the Jura's bark-wrapped winter cheeses, Vacherin Mont d'Or and Försterkäse. It's made only in winter, when the Ayrshires give rich, hay-fed raw milk, wrapped in spruce cambium (the tree's flexible inner bark) harvested from the farm's own woods, and washed in a cultured brine. At 60 days it's spoonable and tastes of bacon, sweet cream and spruce. It's named for the corner of Caspian Lake where the founders' grandfather had an ice-fishing shack. ACS Best in Show, 2013. Old form, Vermont move, the experience (peel back the top, use a spoon) and the season are all on one page.
- https://www.jasperhillfarm.com/winnimere

**Harbison (Jasper Hill).** The same spruce-bark wrap, put on a soft bloomy-rind cheese. It ripens to spoonable and tastes of sweet cream, button mushroom, woods, a little lemon and mustard. Named for Anne Harbison, the grandmother of Greensboro. A familiar treatment on an unfamiliar cheese, with clear instructions: peel the bark when young, spoon from the top when ripe.
- https://www.jasperhillfarm.com/harbison

**Savage (von Trapp Farmstead).** Two traditions in one wheel: made like a Gruyère or Comté, salted like a cheddar. The eater can taste both parents, cheddar's sharpness and the Alpine sweetness, broth and caramelized onion. The natural invitation is a side-by-side with a Comté and an aged cheddar. Sources as under The Make.

**Shepsog (Grafton Village Cheese).** Half raw sheep's milk, half raw cow's milk, aged at least seven months. Shepsog is the Algonquin word for sheep, which covered Vermont's hills during the 19th-century wool boom before the forest came back. Grafton describes a dusty gray rind, a firm texture that echoes the curd, cultured butter and buttermilk on the nose, and lemony, sweet, caramel and nutty flavors. Saxelby tastes cheddar, baked potato and green grass. It has won first place at the American Cheese Society in a class for cheddars made from mixed and other milks. A new milk, a lost landscape, and a category surprise.

Grafton's published materials don't describe a Dutch or Gouda-style technique. One retailer reads Basque, Alpine, cheddar and aged-Gouda influences in it, and calls the milk thermized rather than raw. Settle both with the maker.
- https://www.graftonvillagecheese.com/?p=349
- https://www.graftonvillagecheese.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/GVC_2024SellSheet_05202024_VFINR.pdf
- https://saxelbycheese.com/products/shepsog
- https://67wine.com/products/grafton-village-shepsog-cave-aged-cheese-vermont

**Pleasant Ridge Reserve (Uplands, Wisconsin).** Beaufort's recipe, changed for a wheel about a ninth the size, made only from pasture milk. A new scale and a new place. Murray's tasting note dares customers to find the pineapple. Sources under The Make and Taste of Place.

## Taste of Place

**Pleasant Ridge Reserve (Uplands).** Made only from milk produced while the cows are out on pasture, and only on days the pasture conditions are right, across about 20 rotated paddocks. Golden color and grassy flavor are the visible and tasteable proof. Named for the ridge the farm sits on.
- https://fairfieldcheese.com/products/pleasant-ridge-reserve
- https://culturecheesemag.com/cheese-library/Pleasant-Ridge-Reserve

**Winnimere (Jasper Hill).** Winter-only, from hay-fed milk, wrapped in bark from the farm's woods. Place shows up in what the cows eat that season and in the trees around the barn. Source under The Reinvention.

**Comté.** Aromas vary with terroir, season of production and time in the cellar, and the trade says so openly. Source under The Make.

## Chosen Ancestors

**Vermont Shepherd.** Cindy Major's "worst cheese ever, like eating a hockey puck," then David Major's trip to the Pyrenees. The journey of choosing, with a place and a reason. (Already in the kit's research.)

**Pleasant Ridge Reserve (Uplands).** After a corporate career, Mike Gingrich set out to make a farmstead cheese modeled on the French Alps, first sowing new grasses, clovers, herbs and wildflowers, then learning Beaufort at the University of Wisconsin and adapting it.
- https://culturecheesemag.com/cheese-library/Pleasant-Ridge-Reserve
- https://cheesemaking.com/blogs/fun-along-the-whey/pleasant-hill-reserve

**Thistle Hill Farm Tarentaise (North Pomfret).** A Savoie cheese chosen because the Alpine climate matches Vermont's (already in the kit's research), made only from the farm's Jersey milk while the cows are on pasture.
- https://www.cornucopia.org/farmer-spotlight/thistle-hill-farm/

## The Inheritance

**Oma (von Trapp Farmstead).** The grandparents, Werner and Erika, moved their six children to the Waitsfield farm in the late 1950s and dairied until 1979. The parents, Martin and Kelly, kept the herd and took it organic. Sebastian and his brother Dan started the creamery in 2009. Oma is named for the grandmother, who championed the farm and the cheese, and who used to ask why they'd named a stinky cheese after her. The joke is what people remember.
- https://culturecheesemag.com/stories/a-vermont-legacy-continued-in-curd/
- https://blog.murrayscheese.com/?p=7665

**Crowley Cheese (Healdville).** A. Winfield Crowley built the factory in 1881 to turn neighbors' surplus milk into cheese, after the family had made cheese from its own milk on a farm up the hill. Three generations of Crowleys ran it, and it still makes cheese with its original methods and recipes in the original building, which is on the National Register of Historic Places.
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crowley_Cheese_Factory

## Why It Tastes Like This

**Savage (von Trapp).** The cows get a varied diet with very little grain and plenty of exercise, so they give less milk, with more flavor. One choice, one consequence.
- https://www.murrayscheese.com/savage

**Pleasant Ridge Reserve (Uplands).** Murray's note: bright and snappy, tropical fruit and butterscotch, a delicate crunch, and the fun of surprising friends by calling out the pineapple. A specific, surprising word gives the buyer something to notice and repeat.
- https://murrayscheese.com/pleasant-ridge-reserve

**Uplands, grass-fed sweetness meeting a savory washed rind.** (Already in the kit's research.)

## The Working Landscape

**Jasper Hill Farm.** Describes itself as a mission-based company working to preserve Vermont's working landscape. The Cellars were built partly to give smaller local makers aging, sales and distribution, lowering the barriers to a national market. The local anchor (the year about a third of Greensboro's dairies sold their cows) is in the kit's research.
- https://jasperhillfarm.com/job-posts/cheesemaking-and-affinage-apprentice
- https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jasper_Hill_Farm

**Von Trapp Farmstead.** Started making cheese to secure a sustainable future for a dairy founded in 1959; the whey feeds the farm's pigs, which are sold at the farm shop.
- https://www.citymarket.coop/market/producer-profiles/von-trapp-farmstead
- https://culturecheesemag.com/stories/a-vermont-legacy-continued-in-curd/

**Grafton Village Cheese.** A business of the nonprofit Windham Foundation; profits go back into supporting Vermont's rural communities.
- https://nfca.coop/cave-to-co-op-november-2022/

## Nobody Does It Alone

**Cabot Clothbound.** Made at the Cabot co-op, sent to the Cellars at Jasper Hill when a few days old, wrapped and aged for months, and tasted batch by batch. A co-op, a cellar, and named people in both.
- https://saxelbycheese.com/pages/library-of-cheese-c

**Oma and Savage (von Trapp).** Oma ripens at the Cellars at Jasper Hill. Savage has been aged in Jasper Hill's Alpine vaults and in Murray's caves. The cellar is a named partner, and the same curd becomes a different cheese in a different cave.
- https://www.citymarket.coop/market/producer-profiles/von-trapp-farmstead
- https://cheesenotes.com/post/80729203366/a-visit-to-von-trapp-farmstead/amp
- https://www.murrayscheese.com/dp/murray-s-cave-aged-reserve-savage

## Second Life

**Grafton Village Cheese.** Founded in 1892 as the Grafton Cooperative Cheese Company to turn local farmers' surplus milk into cheese. A fire destroyed the factory; the Windham Foundation restored the company in the mid-1960s. The name and the village are the bridge.
- https://culturecheesemag.com/cheese-library/Shepsog

**Shepsog (Grafton).** The lost thing is a landscape: the sheep that covered Vermont's hills before the forest came back. Sheep's milk returns, as a cheese named for them. Sources under The Reinvention.
