# Photo Essay

**Purpose:** Eight to twelve phone photos, shot over a week or a season, that carry the creamery's one approved story in order, each with a short caption.

## Who reads it and how

Two readers. First, whoever on the farm holds the phone: busy, not a photographer, shooting between chores. Second, the viewer: swiping an Instagram carousel (a few seconds a slide), scrolling the website story page, or standing at a printed board in the farm store. All skimmers. The photos do the work; each caption answers the question its photo raises.

The research is why this piece exists. In a packaging experiment, a picture of the producer raised perceived quality and authenticity, while a paragraph about the producer had the opposite effect. In restaurant field experiments, letting customers see the cooks at work raised reported quality by 22%. Show the person, show the work, keep the words short.

## What it needs from what the maker has gathered

The approved story and its essential sentence; the places and actions on the farm (milking, make room, cave, store); confirmed story elements, especially The Hands, This Wheel This Season, What We Won't Do, and The Living Thing; recurring events that can only be shot when they happen; and the maker's preferences (no children's faces, no photos of a certain building).

## Structure

1. **Opening photo.** Makes someone stop or swipe. From the story, not a beauty shot. Caption under 15 words: the story's question or turning point.
2. **The shot list** (8 to 12 photos in all, in story order). For each:
   - **In frame** (one line).
   - **Carries** (planning only): which beat or element.
   - **Photo note** a non-photographer can follow ("shoot from shelf height").
   - **Caption** (10 to 40 words, public-facing).
   - **When:** "any day this week," or a moment from part 4.
   At least one photo shows the maker's face, mid-task. At least two show hands at a specific task, with a time or number in the caption.
3. **Closing photo.** The cheese cut, on a table, ready to share. Caption: the For the Table line, then where to buy.
4. **Capture when it happens** (two to four moments). Seasonal or once-a-year beats: first day on pasture, first make of the season, last wheel before winter, a calving. Say what to shoot and why it strengthens the story. Until then, the essay runs on ordinary-day photos.
5. **Where it lives.**
   - *Instagram carousel:* up to 20 slides allowed; 8 to 12 is plenty. Shoot vertical, crop every photo to 4:5 (the first slide's shape sets all of them). Post caption under 150 words; put each photo's caption in its alt text.
   - *Website:* one photo per screen, caption beneath.
   - *Farm store board:* 8 to 10 prints at least 8 x 10 inches, captions readable standing.

Total caption text: about 150 to 350 words.

## Photo notes for the person holding the phone

Include these in every essay, adapted to the farm.

- **Light:** Window light and overcast days. Light falls on the work, not behind it. No flash in the cave or make room.
- **Hands:** Close enough that hands and what they touch fill the frame. Wet, scarred, gloved, curd-covered hands are better than clean ones.
- **Height:** Shoot from the height of the work (the vat rim, the shelf, the cow's head), not standing and looking down.
- **No posed smiles:** Ask the person to keep working and ignore you. Take ten frames; keep the one where they forgot you. Eyes on the work beat eyes on the camera.
- **Steady:** Wipe the lens, brace your elbows, tap to focus on what matters. No filters or staged props. A hose on the floor belongs in the make room.

## Rules

- **One story.** Every photo carries a beat of the approved story. A farm tour is not an essay.
- **Captions are public-facing:** only maker-confirmed or sourced facts. Gaps look like `[ask: how many wheels are on these shelves right now?]`. Never invent a number, name, or quote.
- **Shot list, photo notes, and part 4 are planning material:** ideas and labeled suggestions are fine.
- Everything outside part 4 must be shootable on an ordinary working day.
- Captions say what the photo can't: the time, the number, the reason. Don't describe what's visible.
- Weak words need something specific behind them; banned words never appear. Honor every maker preference.

## Weak and strong

Weak:
> 1. Sunset over the farm. *"Nestled in the rolling hills of Vermont."* 2. The family, arms crossed, smiling at the camera. *"Our passion is cheese."* 3. A wheel on slate with grapes. *"Handcrafted artisan goodness."*

Nothing is happening, faces are posed, captions are claims with nothing behind them, and no photo carries a story beat.

Strong (fictional Birch Hollow Creamery, excerpt):
> **1. Opening.** The make-room calendar, May and October circled. *Note:* fill the frame, window light from the side. *Caption:* "We only make Hollow Tomme between these two circles." *When:* any day.
> **2.** The pasture gate opening, Jerseys walking through. *Carries:* season, herd. *Note:* crouch to cow-head height, brace on the gatepost. *Caption:* "From May to October our 22 Jerseys are on grass. Every wheel comes from that milk." *When:* first morning on pasture; until then, any summer morning.
> **4.** Ana's hands flipping a 4-lb wheel on a cave shelf. *Carries:* The Hands. *Note:* shelf height, no flash. *Caption:* "Every wheel is turned by hand, once a week, for at least five months."
> **5.** Ana in profile, reading a rind. *Carries:* a face. *Note:* let her keep working. *Caption:* `[ask: what are you looking for when you check a rind?]`
> **7.** The empty make room. *Carries:* What We Won't Do. *Caption:* "In 2019 we stopped making cheese in winter. That year we lost two wholesale accounts. Last year both came back." *When:* November.
> **10. Closing.** A wedge on rye bread with honey, dry cider beside it. *Caption:* `[For the Table line]` `[ask: where can people buy it?]`
> **Capture when it happens:** The first morning back on pasture. It's the story's turning point and happens once a year.

## Self-check

- Does every photo carry a beat of the one story, in order?
- Could someone shoot everything outside part 4 this week?
- Is there a real face mid-task and at least two photos of hands at work?
- Does every caption fact trace to the maker or a source, with gaps where missing?
- Is caption text under about 350 words?
